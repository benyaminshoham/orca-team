# Product discovery and MVP scope

## Purpose

Define the smallest coherent release hypothesis that can deliver and measure value while retiring material uncertainty.

## Triggers

Project binding; solution-first request; weak or conflicting evidence; scope decision; new learning; product acceptance.

## Required inputs

Approved brief, user/stakeholder evidence, policy/business intent, current journey and alternatives, constraints, existing product/data evidence, feasibility input, risk appetite, and decision authority.

## Workflow

1. Frame the problem as user/context/need/obstacle/outcome; separate it from the proposed solution.
2. Inventory evidence and assumptions. Assess recency, relevance, coverage, bias, and unanswered high-impact questions.
3. Map the whole journey, actors, channels, constraints, existing alternatives, and failure consequences.
4. Define outcome measures with baseline, desired direction, collection method, guardrails, owner, and review point.
5. Rank assumptions by impact and uncertainty; choose research, prototype, data analysis, or technical spike proportional to the decision.
6. Generate options and test riskiest assumptions before committing irreversible implementation.
7. Define the MVP as end-to-end slices; include essential trust, accessibility, recovery, measurement, operations, and explicit exclusions.
8. Write acceptance examples and obtain feasibility, risk, testability, and human scope review.

## Decision rules

- Prioritize user outcome and learning value, not feature count or stakeholder volume.
- Do not treat stakeholder assertion, analytics correlation, or prototype preference as conclusive user evidence.
- Stop or pivot when evidence shows the problem is not valuable, feasible, lawful, or suitable for this product.
- Defer optional breadth before degrading a critical journey.

## Output contract

Problem statement, evidence/assumption map, journey and actor map, outcome measures, option rationale, prioritized MVP slices, exclusions, acceptance examples, research/measurement plan, and decision log.

## Quality checklist

Users and context are specific; evidence supports the problem; success is observable; risks are front-loaded; MVP is coherent and bounded; exclusions and unknowns are visible; every slice is testable.

## Failure and escalation conditions

Escalate inaccessible users/evidence, conflicting decision owners, illegal or harmful intent, unmeasurable outcomes, hard constraints that invalidate value, or scope incompatible with capacity.

## Collaboration touchpoints

Engineering assesses feasibility; Platform/Security assesses data and operational risk; QA challenges testability; PM records decisions and dependencies; humans approve intent and scope.
