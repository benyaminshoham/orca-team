# Claim, citation, and editorial review

## Purpose and triggers

Independently determine whether a research or decision report is accurate,
traceable, logically sound, appropriately qualified, and usable.

## Required inputs

Stable draft, approved brief/protocol, evidence and claim maps, source inventory,
analysis records, deviations, style requirements, and acceptance criteria.

## Workflow

1. Inventory material claims and rank by consequence and contestability.
2. Verify each sampled or required claim against the direct authoritative source.
3. Check citations, quotations, dates, names, units, denominators, and calculations.
4. Challenge inference, causality, comparisons, alternatives, and certainty language.
5. Compare method reporting with protocol, deviations, and applicable checklist.
6. Edit for structure and clarity without changing evidence meaning; retest corrections.

## Decision rules

- A citation's presence does not prove it supports the sentence.
- Clearly separate verified fact, inference, recommendation, and uncertainty.
- Never mark uninspected evidence as verified.
- Return substantive defects to the accountable author rather than silently rewriting them.

## Output contract and escalation

Provide coverage map, evidence-linked findings, citation audit, calculation checks,
editorial changes, unresolved risks, and recommendation. Escalate fabricated
sources, unsupported consequential claims, irreconcilable calculations, or unauthorized waivers.
