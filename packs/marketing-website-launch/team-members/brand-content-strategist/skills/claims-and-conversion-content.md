# Claims and conversion content

## Purpose

Produce persuasive, clear website copy while keeping factual claims, disclosures, testimonials, and conversion choices evidence-backed and non-deceptive.

## Triggers

Use for headlines, proof points, pricing, comparisons, guarantees, statistics, testimonials, endorsements, reviews, calls to action, forms, disclosures, and final content QA.

## Required inputs

Approved page briefs; claims evidence; product/pricing facts; brand voice; permissions; material relationships; offer terms; required disclosures; form/data purpose; authorized approvers.

## Workflow

1. Create a claims register with exact proposed wording, likely audience interpretation, evidence, scope/conditions, disclosure, owner, approval, and review date.
2. Classify higher-risk claims: objective performance, comparative/superiority, quantified result, health/safety, financial, environmental, price/savings, guarantee, testimonial, or endorsement.
3. Draft the clearest supportable expression. Keep qualifications close, prominent, understandable, and consistent with the main claim.
4. Draft page copy in hierarchy: descriptive title and heading, audience problem/context, value, proof, action, and necessary decision information.
5. Make CTAs specific about outcome. For forms, explain purpose and requested data; avoid preselection or asymmetry that undermines choice.
6. Verify testimonials/reviews reflect authorized source material, preserve material context, identify atypical-result risk, and disclose material connections where required.
7. Review copy in representative responsive designs and implementation; check truncation, accessible names/context, error/help text, and disclosure proximity.
8. Route legal or regulated questions to the named human approver and record disposition.

## Decision rules

- Never strengthen a claim beyond its evidence or imply evidence through imagery/layout that words avoid.
- Silence can mislead; include material limitations needed for a reasonable decision.
- Do not fabricate or composite testimonials, results, scarcity, popularity, credentials, or endorsements.
- Treat AI-generated copy as a draft requiring the same source and approval controls.
- Do not make consent or rejection harder, less visible, or more confusing than acceptance.

## Output contract

Production copy; claims register; disclosures; CTA and form microcopy; metadata copy; source/permission status; content QA result; unresolved risks and approvers.

## Quality checklist

Claims are truthful, supportable, scoped, current, and non-contradictory; disclosures are clear and proximate; actions are understandable; voice is consistent; page meaning survives responsive presentation.

## Failure and escalation conditions

Block publication of claims with missing evidence/permission or rejected approval. Escalate regulated claims, legal ambiguity, deceptive interaction requests, or irreconcilable brand and clarity requirements.

## Collaboration touchpoints

Designer preserves hierarchy and disclosure prominence; engineer preserves copy and accessible labeling; reviewer independently checks content and presentation; PM records approval and blocks.
