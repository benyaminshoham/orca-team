# Message and content strategy

## Purpose

Create an evidence-led message architecture and page system that connects audience needs, business intent, and usable website content.

## Triggers

Use during discovery, sitemap/page-brief creation, content revision, localization planning, or when business, audience, offer, or brand inputs change.

## Required inputs

Approved brief; audience and customer evidence; offer/product facts; brand strategy and voice; current content; page inventory; search intent inputs; legal/privacy constraints; decision owners.

## Workflow

1. Inventory sources and distinguish observed evidence, approved facts, hypotheses, and gaps.
2. Define priority audiences by context, need, barrier, desired outcome, and proof required; avoid unsupported personas.
3. State the core value proposition and supporting messages. Map each to evidence, audience relevance, and prohibited overreach.
4. Map priority journeys and assign each page one primary purpose, audience question, desired action, and proof role.
5. Collaborate with the designer on sitemap, labels, page hierarchy, cross-links, and reusable module purposes.
6. Draft page briefs: entry context, key message, sequence, proof, objections, CTA, content modules, media, metadata intent, and measurement intent.
7. Define voice rules through usable contrasts and examples tied to the approved brand; account for clarity, inclusive language, translation, and scanning.
8. Validate coverage, duplication, orphan content, terminology, content ownership, and approval state. Version the system when inputs change.

## Decision rules

- Prefer audience evidence and approved product truth over fashionable phrasing.
- One page may support several needs but must have one primary purpose.
- Preserve meaning and evidence when shortening copy; never trade truth for conversion pressure.
- Search language may clarify audience vocabulary but cannot dictate misleading or low-value content.
- Separate reusable message rules from page-specific production copy.

## Output contract

Source/gap inventory; audience-needs summary; message and proof architecture; journey/content map; sitemap contributions; terminology and voice rules; page briefs; content inventory/status; open decisions.

## Quality checklist

- Every core message is relevant, differentiated, understandable, and traceable.
- Every page earns its place and supports an explicit journey.
- Navigation and headings use audience language consistently.
- Copy requirements include proof, disclosure, media, and accessibility needs.
- Unknowns and approval owners are visible.

## Failure and escalation conditions

Escalate absent product truth, contradictory decision owners, missing brand authority, unsupported segmentation, unclear offer, or scope that requires new research/identity work.

## Collaboration touchpoints

The designer co-develops information hierarchy; engineer confirms content-model and rendering constraints; reviewer challenges clarity and implementation; PM controls acceptance and changes.
