# Project binding and intake

## Purpose

Bind this reusable team to a concrete website launch without changing the pack. The resulting project brief is the execution source of truth and must distinguish supplied facts, approved decisions, assumptions, and unresolved questions.

## Execution-time questions

### Outcomes and authority

- What business outcome should the site influence, and which measures are decision-useful?
- Who owns brand, content, technical, legal/privacy, release, and final acceptance decisions?
- What are the launch date, milestone constraints, capacity, budget, and risk tolerance?
- What is explicitly out of scope, and what evidence defines done?

### Audience, offer, and content

- Which audience segments and priority journeys matter most?
- What offer and actions should each page support?
- Which brand, research, product, pricing, and claim sources are authoritative?
- Which claims require evidence, qualification, expiry review, or human approval?
- Which pages, assets, languages, testimonials, endorsements, and disclosures are required?

### Experience and accessibility

- Which devices, browsers, assistive technologies, and network conditions matter?
- What accessibility standard and level applies, and is a formal conformance statement expected?
- Which media, animation, forms, interactive elements, and third-party widgets are planned?
- Is an approved design system available, or must this project define a limited web system?

### Technology and release

- Is this a new site, migration, or incremental release?
- What domain, URL history, CMS/framework, repository, hosting, environments, and integrations apply?
- Who controls DNS, deployment, backups, rollback, analytics, consent, forms, and operational monitoring?
- What performance targets, security policies, browser matrix, and release windows apply?

### Search, measurement, privacy, and compliance

- What current URLs, redirects, search baselines, priority queries, and indexing constraints exist?
- What data is collected, why, where it flows, who can access it, and how long it is retained?
- Which analytics, advertising, personalization, cookies, or session tools are proposed?
- Which jurisdictions, industry rules, contracts, privacy notices, and advertising requirements apply?

## Binding rules

1. Inventory every supplied source with owner, date/version, authority, and conflicts.
2. Record unanswered material questions as blockers unless an authorized human approves a bounded assumption.
3. Select conditional variants from the manifest and name any external specialists or approvals they activate.
4. Define artifact locations, release scope, acceptance evidence, and decision owners.
5. Establish a claims register and data-flow inventory before approving affected content or integrations.
6. Do not treat competitor content, search results, analytics, generated text, or prior-site copy as automatically accurate or authorized.
7. Freeze an approved brief version for planning; route later changes through change control.

## Required project-brief sections

Context and outcomes; scope and exclusions; users and journeys; offer and claims; content inventory; experience and accessibility; technology and operations; search and measurement; privacy/compliance; variants; roles and authority; timeline and capacity; gates and definition of done; source inventory; assumptions; risks and unresolved decisions; approvals.
